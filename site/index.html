Sustained optimization across the AI infrastructure stack

Making AI systems faster requires repeated implementation, profiling, and validation. A change must preserve correctness, obey numerical and hardware constraints, and improve performance on the actual workload.

Infrathon studies whether language agents can sustain this process over long horizons. Its tasks span training and inference, from GPU kernels and KV-cache policies to complete training loops and serving systems. Agents develop candidates with public tests and receive correctness and performance feedback from private tests.

Task Definition

10 training tasks and 15 inference tasks, organized by what the agent can change.

11 tasks · 12 hours

Operators

Optimize individual computation or data-movement primitives.

Quantized attention, grouped GEMM, and KV block transfer.

5 tasks · 24 hours

Modules

Improve a functional component within a fixed workflow.

Training objectives, KV eviction and scoring, and KV compression.

9 tasks · 24 hours

Systems

Optimize complete loops, pipelines, or serving orchestration.

Online distillation, video generation, and concurrent agent serving.

Every task specifies a workspace, public tests, private tests, resources, and a time budget. The budget includes tool execution and feedback waits.

Task Execution

A feedback loop separates candidate development from private evaluation.

Work environment

The agent edits candidates, runs public correctness checks, and profiles performance.

Controller

Snapshots the candidate and checks task rules and the allowed editable scope.

Judge environment

Runs private tests and returns validity checks, scores, and permitted diagnostics.

The agent uses this feedback to continue optimizing. Stop hooks and session recovery sustain execution through the experimental budget; the framework also supports runs without a preset deadline.

Key Findings

Final performance, time to improve, and reliable testing tell different parts of the story.

Strong overall results, task-specific winners

Claude leads on 17 tasks, Astra on six, and Sol and Qwen on one each. The best model still depends on the workload.

Many runs improve in the second half

Of full-budget runs with a midpoint score, 91.6% improve later. Gains range from small refinements to larger advances.
